Research02 of 07

Two problems, two scales.

Molecular dynamics of solder reliability, and latent heat thermal energy storage.

Undergraduate thesis, in progress

Atomistic molecular dynamics of thermomechanical reliability in copper, SAC305 and copper solder joint stacks

Jul 2026 to present. Supervisor: Dr. Mohammad Abdul Motalab, Dept. of ME, BUET

Solder joints are a frequent point of failure in electronic packages. I model the SAC305 tin, silver and copper solder alloy with its Ag3Sn and Cu6Sn5 intermetallic precipitates built as discrete particles, instead of the randomly substituted atoms used in earlier models, and study how that microstructure changes the solder's response to monotonic and cyclic shear.

The next stage extends the model to a full layered stack of copper, intermetallic compounds and solder, to examine how damage develops at the interfaces.

Ongoing

Honeycomb latent heat thermal energy storage

Oct 2025 to present. Dept. of ME, BUET

A thermal storage unit for solar-assisted domestic hot water, using paraffin phase change material sealed in honeycomb cells. It began as our ME 310 design project. I designed the unit, led the fabrication of the prototype, used transient computational fluid dynamics in ANSYS Fluent to follow the melting and solidification fronts, and wrote the conference paper. Our team also co-authored and submitted a proposal to the ASHRAE Undergraduate Program Equipment Grant for a teaching version of the unit.
