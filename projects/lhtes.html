Project01 of 04

Honeycomb latent heat thermal energy storage

A storage module that holds solar heat in melting paraffin wax, so hot water is ready in the morning and evening, long after the sun has peaked.

My roleLead designer, fabrication lead, corresponding author
ContextME 310 design project, BUET
PaperASHRAE 2027 Winter Conference, under review
ToolsSolidWorks, ANSYS Fluent, workshop fabrication

The problem01

Sun at noon, showers at dawn.

Solar heat arrives when households need it least. Storage has to carry it across the day.

Solar heat collectedHot water drawnIllustrative
Water tanks are bulkyStoring heat as warm water works, but it needs a lot of volume.
Melting wax is denseParaffin stores far more heat per volume as it melts, right at hot water temperatures.
But it conducts poorlyThe real design problem is moving heat in and out of the wax fast enough.

The design02

Why a honeycomb.

Paraffin sits in sealed hexagonal cells. Hot water flows through the gaps between them.

Tiles with no dead spaceHexagons pack without voids, so no volume is wasted between cells.
Thin walls that carry loadEvery wall is shared and loaded in its own plane, so thin sheet works without stiffeners.
Gaps become channelsThe even gap between neighbouring cells is the water passage. No extra structure needed.
One lid closes every cellCells are as tall as the shell, so the top and bottom plates seal them all at once.
An honest caveat from the paper: hexagons are not the best shape for heat transfer area. They were chosen because they can be rolled and welded from plain sheet in a small workshop.
