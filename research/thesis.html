The problem01

Every power cycle is a small shear.

A chip and its circuit board expand by different amounts when they heat up. The solder joint between them gets sheared every time, and damage starts where solder meets copper.

Swipe to see the zoomed view

TinSilver-rich particleCopper and copper-rich phasesIllustrative
Heat cycles repeatDevices warm and cool thousands of times over their life.
Mismatch shears the jointDifferent expansion above and below the solder loads it in shear.
Too small to seeThe damage begins at a scale that continuum models cannot resolve, so I simulate the atoms.

The idea02

Build what is really there.

Earlier atomistic models of SAC305 scatter silver and copper atoms at random through the tin. Real SAC305 does not look like that: its silver and copper sit inside discrete intermetallic particles. I build those particles explicitly.

Earlier modelsRandom substitutionSilver and copper atoms replace tin at random sites. Simple to build, but not how the alloy forms.
This workDiscrete precipitatesThe same silver and copper, gathered into real intermetallic particles inside a tin matrix.

The building blocks

Each block is relaxed on its own first, then carved into particles. Rendered in OVITO.

The cells03

What the real models look like.

Spherical intermetallic particles sit inside a tin matrix. I build several cells with different particle placements, so no conclusion rests on one lucky arrangement.

Atoms coloured by atomic volume. Particles that cross a cell face wrap to the opposite face, because the cell repeats in every direction.

The method04

From atoms to answers.

Every simulation follows the same eight steps.

  1. Step 1Choose the physicsLAMMPS with a published Ag-Cu-Sn interatomic potential.
  2. Step 2Relax the blocksEach intermetallic relaxed on its own.
  3. Step 3Build the cellCarve particles and place them in tin.
  4. Step 4Minimise energyRemove overlaps before anything moves.
  5. Step 5EquilibrateBring the cell to room temperature and pressure.
  6. Step 6Shear itTilt the cell steadily in one direction.
  7. Step 7Cycle itShear back and forth, like repeated heating.
  8. Step 8AnalyseRead stiffness, strength and energy loss.
